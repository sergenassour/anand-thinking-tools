# Narrative structures library

Eleven structures compiled for the workshop, in the presenter's order of usefulness (STAR first). Each has
a "use it when" tip in the top-right of its slide, the steps, why it works, what it is best for, and ten
practice questions: five work questions (generated with ChatGPT) on the left and five personal questions
(written by the presenter) on the right. Practise on the personal ones; they are more fun and the
structure sticks faster.

How to use this file: pick the structure whose "use it when" matches the moment, map the user's content
onto the steps, and produce a storyboard before anything else (see SKILL.md, Tool 6).

## 01. STAR

**Origin**: structured behavioural interviewing, industrial-organisational psychology; adopted by Amazon,
Google and the Big Four.

**Use it when**: someone ambushes you with "tell me about a time when...". The presenter's personal
favourite and default.

**Steps**
1. **Situation**: set the scene in one sentence: where, when, what was at stake.
2. **Task / Thinking**: say what you had to achieve and how you weighed it up.
3. **Action**: describe what you actually did, in the first person.
4. **Result**: quantify the outcome and say what it proved.

**Why it works**: turns claims into evidence. The audience hears proof of judgement and impact rather
than opinion.

**Best for**: interviews, performance reviews, credibility-building anecdotes, evidence of personal impact,
and (as demonstrated) a consulting proposal that needs to read as experience rather than theory.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| What does a typical day look like for you in your current role? | When you're not working, how do you most like to spend your time? |
| What sparked your initial interest in this career path? | Have you found any cool hidden spots or great places to eat in this neighbourhood? |
| What inspired you to attend this particular event today? | What is something in your life that you are really looking forward to right now? |
| What is the highest leverage opportunity you are currently facing in your business or career? | What has been the highlight of your week so far? |
| Where do you see your industry heading in the next five years? | What are you currently reading, or what's the last great show you binge-watched? |

## 02. SCQA

**Origin**: Barbara Minto, McKinsey (the Pyramid Principle school).

**Use it when**: you want to sound like you have done this before. Describe the situation, the
complication, the question that needs addressing, then the answer.

**Steps**
1. **Situation**: the stable, agreed starting point everyone recognises.
2. **Complication**: what changed or went wrong that makes the status quo untenable.
3. **Question**: the single question the complication forces.
4. **Answer**: your response to that question, then the supporting detail.

**Why it works**: the audience arrives at the question themselves before you answer it, so the answer
lands as inevitable rather than imposed.

**Best for**: proposals, briefs, executive memos, the opening of almost any strategy document.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Why does your team need more budget? | Why do you need a bigger apartment? |
| What is the biggest risk in your plan? | Why go somewhere new for the holidays? |
| Why enter this market now, not later? | Why is it time to replace your car? |
| What changed in your industry this year? | Why should you pick the restaurant? |
| Why might your best customer leave? | Why do you need to get a cat? |

Note: the SCQA definition slide was not photographed; steps above follow Minto's published framework and
the presenter's spoken description. The practice questions are from the slide.

## 03. Pyramid Principle

**Origin**: Barbara Minto (McKinsey).

**Use it when**: the CEO has already checked their watch.

**Steps**
1. **Answer first**: lead with the recommendation in one sentence.
2. **Key reasons**: give the two or three reasons it holds.
3. **Evidence**: back each reason with data or analysis, one layer down.

**Why it works**: respects executive time and creates immediate clarity and authority.

**Best for**: board-level communication, strategy updates, recommendations.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Should we hire two more people? | Which is the best city you have lived in? |
| What should we do about pricing? | What film should everyone watch? |
| Which project should we stop? | Should people tip in restaurants? |
| Should the team return to the office? | Is a gym membership worth the money? |
| What about our slowest-paying client? | When is the best time to take vacation? |

## 04. PREP

**Use it when**: you have eight seconds and an opinion.

**Steps**
1. **Point**: state your position in a single sentence.
2. **Reason**: give the one reason that matters most.
3. **Example**: offer a concrete case or number.
4. **Point**: restate the position to close.

**Why it works**: forces clarity, brevity, and a strong takeaway.

**Best for**: short persuasive answers, interviews, panels, Q&A.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Are long meetings ever worth it? | Is breakfast the best meal of the day? |
| Is remote work good for new joiners? | Are cats better than dogs? |
| Should managers come from the team? | Is Dubai the best city in the world? |
| Is customer feedback overrated? | Should soccer have a salary cap? |
| Does your industry need regulation? | Is it ever acceptable to be late? |

## 05. A3 / PDCA logic

**Origin**: Toyota / Lean problem-solving tradition. The presenter had to use it at Sony.

**Use it when**: someone asks whether you actually fixed it.

**Steps**
1. **Background**: why this matters now.
2. **Current condition**: what is happening today, with numbers.
3. **Problem and root cause**: the gap, and why it exists.
4. **Countermeasures**: what you propose to change.
5. **Implementation**: who does what, by when.
6. **Results / follow-up**: what changed, and what happens next.

**Why it works**: shows extensive disciplined thinking and credibility in execution-heavy environments.

**Best for**: operational, process, and transformation topics.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Why does your report arrive late? | Why is your kitchen always a mess? |
| Why are customer complaints rising? | Why do you skip the gym? |
| Why did the last project overrun? | Why is your personal battery dead by 3pm? |
| Why is turnover highest in one team? | Why do you leave late for the airport? |
| Why does onboarding take six weeks? | Why do you keep losing your keys? |

## 06. Monroe's Motivated Sequence

**Origin**: Alan H. Monroe, Purdue University (1930s).

**Use it when**: you need people to do something, not just nod.

**Steps**
1. **Attention**: open with something that stops the room.
2. **Need**: show the problem and why it is theirs.
3. **Satisfaction**: present your solution plainly.
4. **Visualisation**: let them picture life after the change.
5. **Action**: make the ask specific.

**Why it works**: built specifically to move audiences from understanding to action.

**Best for**: persuasive, change-oriented, or sales-style talks.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Get the board to fund your idea. | Get a friend to run a race with you. |
| Get your team onto a new system. | Sell a holiday without phones. |
| Ask a client for a three-year deal. | Start a padel club with neighbours. |
| Make the case to cut a popular product. | Get a friend to leave a job they hate. |
| Get your peers to share their data. | Get rid of the family television. |

## 07. PIIR

**Origin**: consulting problem-solving practice.

**Use it when**: you are being paid to have a view.

**Steps**
1. **Problem**: state the issue in the audience's terms.
2. **Insight**: reveal what the analysis actually shows.
3. **Implication**: spell out what it means for the business.
4. **Recommendation**: what should be done.

**Why it works**: positions you as someone who brings insight, not just information.

**Best for**: consulting-style, insight-driven presentations.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| What do your sales numbers tell us? | What does your bank statement say? |
| What is behind your falling margins? | What has your fitness tracker taught you? |
| What did your last three losses show? | What does your watch or handbag history reveal? |
| What does churn say about the product? | What do your last five arguments show? |
| Which number does leadership misread? | What does your weekend say about you? |

## 08. Before-After-Bridge

**Origin**: copywriting and change-communication practice.

**Use it when**: you are selling change to people who like things as they are.

**Steps**
1. **Before**: describe today's situation honestly.
2. **After**: paint the improved state in concrete terms.
3. **Bridge**: how you get from one to the other.

**Why it works**: simple, visual, and emotionally intuitive.

**Best for**: change, transformation, vision talks.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Sell your team a new way of working. | Get a friend to change their diet. |
| Why does your department need change? | Sell the family on moving villa. |
| Pitch your product to a sceptic. | Persuade someone to learn a language. |
| Make the case for automating a task. | Make the case for quitting social media. |
| Explain the value of your training. | Get a friend to take up a sport. |

## 09. What? So what? Now what?

**Origin**: reflective practice (Rolfe et al.).

**Use it when**: your slide is forty numbers and no point. Forty data points with no insight is a data
dump, not a presentation.

**Steps**
1. **What?**: the facts, briefly.
2. **So what?**: why those facts matter to this audience.
3. **Now what?**: decisions, actions, next steps.

**Why it works**: prevents data dumps and forces strategic relevance.

**Best for**: analysis, updates, reflective or strategic discussions.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Report on last quarter's results. | Tell us about your last holiday. |
| Brief us on a competitor's move. | Report on last night's dinner. |
| Summarise the customer survey. | How did your team play at the weekend? |
| Update the board on a slipped project. | What happened at the family gathering? |
| Debrief the team after a failed launch. | Review the last thing you bought. |

## 10. Case-Cause-Consequence-Cure (4C)

**Use it when**: something has gone wrong and everyone is looking at you.

**Steps**
1. **Case**: lay out the situation as it stands.
2. **Cause**: explain what is driving it.
3. **Consequence**: state what happens if nothing changes.
4. **Cure**: what we should do.

**Why it works**: builds urgency and decision momentum.

**Best for**: risk, performance issues, turnaround situations.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| Your best salesperson has resigned. | Your flight has been cancelled. |
| A supplier has raised prices by 20%. | Dinner has burned and guests arrive. |
| Two departments have stopped talking. | You are double-booked on Saturday. |
| A launch has been delayed twice. | Your car will not start on Monday. |
| Costs are up and revenue is flat. | Your phone fell in the pool. |

## 11. Story Spine

**Origin**: Kenn Adams / Pixar narrative structure, adapted for strategy.

**Use it when**: the strategy is sound but nobody can remember it.

**Steps**
1. **Once upon a time...**: the world as it was.
2. **Every day...**: the routine that held.
3. **Until one day...**: the disruption that broke it.
4. **Because of that...**: the consequences that followed.
5. **Until finally...**: the resolution and new direction.

**Why it works**: makes strategy and change feel human and memorable without losing structure.

**Best for**: strategy narratives, change stories, origin stories, town halls.

**Practice questions**

| Work | Personal |
|---|---|
| How your company started. | How you met your closest friend. |
| Your best client relationship. | Your worst holiday. |
| Your team's biggest turnaround. | A skill you taught yourself. |
| A product that nearly failed. | The best meal you ever ate. |
| Your own career so far. | A risk that paid off. |

## 12. Quick chooser

| The moment | Structure |
|---|---|
| Prove you have done it, with evidence | STAR |
| Open a proposal so the answer feels inevitable | SCQA |
| Executive has no time, wants the recommendation | Pyramid Principle |
| One opinion, eight seconds, a panel or Q&A | PREP |
| Operational fix, someone asks "did you actually fix it" | A3 / PDCA |
| Move people to act, not nod | Monroe |
| You are paid to have a view on the data | PIIR |
| Sell change to people comfortable with today | Before-After-Bridge |
| Avoid a data dump, force the point | What / So what / Now what |
| Crisis, everyone looking at you | 4C |
| Make a sound strategy memorable | Story Spine |

When two fit, prefer the one whose steps match the audience's own questions. If the audience will ask
"so what did you do", STAR. If they will ask "what should we do", Pyramid or PIIR. If they will ask
"why should I care", Monroe or Before-After-Bridge.
